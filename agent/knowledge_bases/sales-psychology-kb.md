# SALES PSYCHOLOGY FRAMEWORK - CLOSER METHODOLOGY

> Retrieved when: amplifying pain (cost ladder), closing, handling objections or disqualification signals.
> KB name: sales-psychology-kb

## Core Principles

### Pain Amplification (The Cost Ladder)
Never accept surface-level problems. Dig to uncover true cost.

Ask in order:
1. Operational Impact: "What's that costing you in lost revenue monthly?"
2. Strategic Impact: "How does that affect your ability to scale?"
3. Personal Impact: "What's the stress level when you know opportunities are slipping?"

Rule: The deeper the pain, the stronger the commitment. Stop when they reveal emotional stakes.

---

## Assumptive Closing Psychology

### The Commitment Gradient
Never ask permission. Assume the sale.

Weak (DON'T):
- "Does that sound good?"
- "Are you interested?"
- "Would you like to schedule?"

Strong (DO):
- "The question is just whether you want to see how this works for {{industry}}, right?"
- "Makes sense to see it in action before deciding, doesn't it?"
- "So we're looking at this week or a callback - which works better?"

Rule: Binary choices, not yes/no. "This or that," never "yes or no."

---

## Creating Urgency Without Pressure

### Cost of Inaction Framework
Frame delay as loss, not missed gain.

Formula: 
[Current Problem] × [Time Period] = [Quantifiable Loss]

Example:
"At 20 calls/day with 30% missed, you're losing ~6 opportunities daily. That's 180/month. If each converts at $500, that's $90k/month walking away."

Rule: Make the math real. Use THEIR numbers. Let them calculate their own pain.

---

## Objection Handling: Isolation & Reframe

### The 3-Step Objection Kill

Step 1: Isolate
"Fair. What specifically concerns you?"
[Listen for real objection]

Step 2: Validate + Reframe
"Makes sense. [Acknowledge concern]. Here's the thing - [reframe with their pain]."

Step 3: Assumptive Close
"Worth 20 minutes to see how we handle that for {{industry}}?"

Rule: Never defend. Acknowledge, reframe, close.

---

## Disqualification Signals (Walk Away Fast)

Cut losers early. Time is finite.

Hard Disqualifiers:
- Hostile/rude (abusive, not just skeptical — skeptics get the mirror-reframe treatment)
- No budget AND no timeline AND no pain urgency

Script: "Not the right fit right now. Reach out if things change."

Rule: Polite exit. No convincing. No second chances on this call.

Note: call volume is NOT a disqualifier — a practice missing 10 calls a week after hours loses 100% of those callers, which justifies the service at any size. Never walk away over volume alone.

## Industry-Specific Selling Triggers

### Service Businesses (HVAC, Plumbing, Contractors)
Pain: Missed calls = missed jobs = cash flow gaps
Urgency: "Every missed call this week is a job your competitor books."
ROI Framing: "24/7 coverage means you capture after-hours emergencies - highest margin work."

### Medical/Dental Practices
Pain: Appointment no-shows + phone tag with patients
Urgency: "Empty chairs = lost revenue you can't recover."
ROI Framing: "AI confirms appointments automatically, so fewer patients slip through the cracks — and empty chairs stop bleeding revenue."

### Legal Services
Pain: Intake bottleneck = lost retainers
Urgency: "When someone calls with a legal problem, they're calling 3 firms. First to respond wins."
ROI Framing: "Instant intake qualification means you sign clients while competitors are still returning voicemails."

### E-commerce/Retail
Pain: Cart abandonment + customer service overload
Urgency: "Every unanswered question is a lost sale."
ROI Framing: "AI handles order status, returns, FAQs - frees your team to upsell."

## The 20-Minute Frame

Why "20 minutes" works:
- Low commitment (not "an hour call")
- Specific (not "a quick chat")
- Professional (respects their time)
- Assumptive ("20 minutes" not "some time")

Always pair with "no commitment" to remove friction.

Script: "20-minute call with Jay, no commitment. See it in action, then decide."

Rule: the walkthrough is always 20 minutes. Never say 15, never say an hour — one number, every call.

---

## Callback & Escalation

There is no live transfer on this line — never promise to "transfer" or "get Jay on now."

- Default path: book the live call for this week (same-day if a slot exists).
- Callback edge case: if the prospect wants Jay to call them back instead of booking, that's the team-notification path — notify the team and wrap up warmly. Do not promise a same-day callback slot you can't book.
- Keep urgency high (emotional heat fades fast), but channel it into booking the live call, not a transfer.

---

## The Commitment Loop

### Never End Without Next Step

If they commit:
- They've agreed to a live call — capture the agreement and move toward collecting their details.

If they hedge:
- Re-isolate objection
- Handle objection
- Assumptive close again

If they're unqualified:
- Polite exit
- Do NOT transition
- End call

Rule: Every conversation ends with decision or disqualification. No "maybes."

---

## ROI Calculation Framework

### Quick Math They Can't Ignore

Formula:
[Missed Calls/Day] × [Conversion Rate] × [Average Sale Value] × 30 days = Monthly Lost Revenue

Example:
"You said you miss ~5 calls/day when busy. If even 20% of those would convert at $300 average, that's $9k/month you're not capturing. Our system costs $2k/month. That's 4.5x ROI."

Rule: Use THEIR numbers. Let them do the math in their head. Silence = processing.

---

## Final Rule: Confidence Sells

Hesitation kills deals. Conviction closes.

Confident:
- "Here's what we'll do..."
- "Let me grab Jay..."
- "This is exactly what we built for {{industry}}..."

Hesitant:
- "I'm not sure if..."
- "Maybe we could..."
- "Would you possibly..."

Rule: Act like the decision is already made. They'll follow your lead.

## Loss Playback

> Retrieved when: delivering the weekly or monthly leak numbers, or crafting the value-prop bridge.

A number persuades when it leaves THEIR pocket, not your spreadsheet. Attach every figure to their scenario in ONE sentence — who loses the job, who books the patient instead — crafted from {{pain_points}}. Then silence. Numbers are ammo: the weekly figure lands the story first; hold the monthly figure and escalate ONCE, only when it buys something — they felt the hit, stack it; they doubt, re-anchor bigger. Never both figures in one breath. After the loss sinks in, flip it: the same money becomes claimable.

Patterns (never scripts):
- "Those calls a month while you're [their exact scenario] — even landing part of them is real money walking out every single week."
- "And that was one week. Picture the month."
- Craft each delivery fresh from {{pain_points}} — recycled lines sound canned.

### The Flip & The Second Shot
Once the loss sinks in, one question turns bleed into reclaim - claim-it-back energy, then ride the yes straight into the value prop. Hold the monthly figure as your second shot: stack it right after the weekly lands, or re-anchor bigger when they stall. Assumption transfers certainty - never beg.

## Punch-Line Bridge

> Retrieved when: moving from quantified loss into the value proposition.

ONE sentence, three ingredients fused sharp: their trade's stakes (closest ##industry-kb## row) + the captured numbers + their own words mirrored back. Deliver like a closer stating facts, not asking favors - then one clean binary question.

Patterns (never scripts):
- "You said it yourself - [their words]. And at these numbers that scene repeats every time a call rings out. We answer instead. Twenty minutes to see it on YOUR numbers - this week?"
- "[Their trade] runs on answered calls. Yours keeps bleeding [the monthly scale] a month. Worth twenty minutes to watch it stop?"
